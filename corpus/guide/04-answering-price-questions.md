# Answering Price Questions Correctly

Notes on how price questions should be handled, and where the masterlist alone
is not enough to answer.

## Always quote the code with the price

A price without a product code is not actionable — the catalog contains many
similarly-named items at different prices. Every quoted price should carry its
product code so the customer can order against it unambiguously.

## Price bands

Items are grouped into bands to make "cheap" and "expensive" answerable:

| Band | Range |
|---|---|
| Budget | under ₱500 |
| Standard | ₱500 – ₱2,000 |
| Mid-range | ₱2,000 – ₱10,000 |
| Premium | ₱10,000 – ₱50,000 |
| Capital equipment | over ₱50,000 |

## When several variants exist

If a customer names a product that exists in multiple sizes, capacities or
chemical types, listing the variants with their prices is more useful than
picking one. Trash bins, fire extinguishers, fire blankets, traffic cones,
life jackets and safety shoes all have multiple variants under similar names.

## Questions the masterlist cannot answer

The masterlist holds item name, colour, code and retail price. It does **not**
hold, and answers should not be invented for:

- **Stock availability or lead time** — not in the masterlist. Refer to the
  sales team.
- **Volume or dealer discount rates** — not in the masterlist. Discounts are
  quoted per transaction.
- **Delivery cost** — depends on destination and quantity.
- **Warranty terms and after-sales service** — not recorded per item.
- **Technical specifications beyond what the item name states** — many items
  carry only a short name with no full spec sheet.
- **Supplier, brand origin, or country of manufacture** — not recorded, except
  where a brand name appears in the item name (YDS, Blue Eagle, Camel, MG).
- **Comparisons of quality between two items** — the masterlist records price,
  not performance. Price band is a rough proxy at best.

For any of these, the correct response is to say the masterlist does not cover
it and direct the customer to the sales team, rather than estimating.

## Known data issues to be careful about

A small number of product codes appear more than once in the source sheet with
different items or different prices attached. Where a lookup returns a code
carrying conflicting data, the answer should state that the masterlist has
conflicting entries for that code and that the price must be confirmed with the
sales team. The affected codes are `FIR-002-50LBS-AFFF`, `LIF-002-05`,
`LIF-001-09`, `LIF-003-01` and `FLA-001-13`.

## Phrasing

Prices should be written in the Philippine convention: `₱2,710.00` or
`PHP 2,710.00`, with a thousands separator. Product codes should be written in
full hyphenated form.
